CHAPTER I



INTRODUCTION

This chapter will present the background and context of the study, highlighting the existing challenges and opportunities within fish market monitoring and management. It will discuss the significance of adopting digital technologies to improve operational efficiency, data management, reporting processes, and decision-making in fish port operations. The chapter will also introduce the research problem and explain the rationale behind the development of the proposed system.

Project Context



Fish ports remain critical economic centers that link fishermen, traders, retailers, and consumers within the marine supply chain. They play a vital role in ensuring national food security and supporting the livelihood of Filipino fishermen along the coasts. However, the majority of public fish ports still rely on manual monitoring systems, traditional handwritten documentation, and fragmented reporting processes all of which contribute to operational inefficiencies and limited data visibility.

Among these is the Lucena Fish Port Complex that will be developed into a regional fish trading center in Southern Luzon. The port will receive daily fish landings from its fleet as well as other fishing activities, and it will serve as a platform for fish products to be transported to retailers and local markets. Although essential for their operation, the identification of fish arrivals, the retailer information, sourcing, supply and reporting will be largely manual or semi-paperless.

Manual logbooks and spreadsheet monitoring can negatively impact operations through late reporting, incomplete data entries, challenges in tracing the origin of the fish supply, or generally making market activities invisible. In addition, administrators may have difficulty in predicting fish availability, the days the session will be busiest, and demand patterns in the absence of foresight.

Recent studies emphasize that incorporating digital tracking systems and standardized data protocols into fisheries supply chains significantly improves transparency, ensures product legitimacy, and streamlines coordination among market players (Pembayun et al., 2024). The adoption of digitally secured monitoring systems across fisheries networks has led to a decrease in manual administrative errors, increasing supply chain accountability and facilitating efficient regulatory compliance (Pratiwi et al., 2024). The continuous flow of real-time supply chain data, when unified under digital monitoring frameworks, provides stakeholders with enhanced visibility that is crucial for managing the safety and quality of perishable food products (Tsolakis et al., 2022).

Predictive analytics and machine learning methodologies have proven highly effective in accurately forecasting market fluctuations and seasonal dynamics, enabling stakeholders to adapt to rapid changes in the fisheries supply chain while optimizing resource allocation (Wu et al., 2024). Furthermore, recent studies demonstrate that integrating data-driven predictive modeling specifically for perishable foods significantly improves supply chain planning, preventing waste by resolving overstocking and understocking issues that frequently occur due to inaccurate demand sensing (Birkmaier et al., 2024). Automating the analysis of complex fisheries data through advanced predictive analytics not only streamlines operations but also provides administrators with the foresight necessary to navigate the unique challenges of the seafood market (Kühn et al., 2024).

The aforementioned studies back the advancement of FishLedger as the digital monitoring and predictive analytics solution enhancing the operational capabilities, transparency, and decisions of those within the LFP. With this, the researchers will develop FishLedger, a progressive web app fish market monitoring system catered for the Lucena Fish Port complex. The system will combine retailer information management, supply source identification, automated reporting, and predictive analytics on a single digital platform. In this way, fish port managers can keep a pulse on routine activity, prepare accurate reports, and predict future supply patterns for improved planning and administration.

Scope and Limitations

This research is the design and development of FishLedger, a progressive web app fish monitoring system developed for Lucena Fish Port Complex. The system facilitates fish port boards to track fish market activities and fish port operation information.

The proposed system incorporates retailer data management, fish supply identification, retail supply source tracing, fish report automation and predictive analytics. These functionalities are designed to strengthen monitoring procedures and provide insight information on the trends of fish supplies and retailers in the fish port.

FishLedger is a web-based fish monitoring software developed for Lucena Fish Port Complex. portrays the functional core of the system in terms of operational data such as fish supplies, retailer information and market monitoring.

Yet there are certain constraints to the system. The system is tailor-made for the Lucena Fish Port Complex and is not applicable to any other fish market in the country which has a different mode of operations. Since it is a web-based system, its effectiveness depends on the accuracy of data encoded by fish port administrators and the consistent participation of fish vendors in the system.  Furthermore, the predicted analytics analysis, which is based on patterns of historical data, does not take into consideration 

the impact of unexpected events such as extreme weather, sudden interruptions of supply or other external factors that might have an influence on the fish market activities.

Definition of Terms

For this study, certain terms will be defined to convey specific meanings as used in the research. These definitions aim to reduce ambiguity and help readers better understand the concepts and system features presented.

Technical Terms

Automated Report Generation – A system feature that will generate summarized reports of fish market activities using recorded data. In this study, it will produce daily, 



weekly, and monthly reports on fish supply transactions, retailer activities, and market operations at the Lucena Fish Port Complex.

Fish Market Monitoring – The process of observing, recording, and tracking fish trading activities, supply movements, and retailer transactions within the fish port. In this study, FishLedger will facilitate this through a centralized digital system.

Predictive Analytics – A branch of data analytics that will use historical data and statistical techniques to forecast future trends or outcomes. In this study, it will be used to predict fish supply and demand patterns.

Retailer Information Management – The process of recording, storing, and maintaining information about fish retailers operating within the fish port, including their profiles, registration details, and transaction records.

Supply Source Identification – The process of identifying and recording the origin of fish supplies delivered to the fish port. The system will track fish supplies from fishing vessels and other sources for proper documentation and traceability.

Dashboard and Data Visualization – A system interface that will present fish market data in graphical and visual formats such as charts and summaries for easier understanding and monitoring.

Operational Terms

Fish Arrival Data – Information recorded in the system regarding the species, volume, and arrival time of fish delivered to the port.

Historical Data – Previously recorded fish supply and market data that will be used by the system for analysis and prediction.

Retailer Transaction Records – Data related to the buying activities of registered retailers within the fish port.

User – Refers to fish port administrators, personnel, and authorized individuals who will encode, manage, and monitor data using the system.

Web-Based System – A software application that will operate through a web browser and require internet connectivity to access its features.

Fish Diversity, Conservation Status, and Market Dynamics

Fish diversity serves as an important indicator of fisheries sustainability and market activity. According to Solante-Catiwalaan and Funesto (2025), public fish markets provide valuable information regarding fish species availability, market demand, conservation status, and economic value. Their study conducted in selected public markets in Cebu City recorded a total of 109 fish species belonging to 35 families, demonstrating the rich diversity of fish species being traded in local markets. The researchers emphasized that monitoring fish species composition is essential for understanding fisheries trends and supporting sustainable fisheries management.

The study revealed that reef-associated fishes accounted for seventy-nine percent (79%) of the species observed in the surveyed markets, while pelagic-neritic, pelagic-oceanic, demersal, and benthopelagic species comprised the remaining portion. This finding highlights the significant contribution of coral reef ecosystems to the fisheries industry and local seafood markets. The researchers noted that the abundance of reef-associated species reflects the importance of marine biodiversity in sustaining fish supply and market operations.

Furthermore, the study identified several commercially important fish families, including Scombridae, Carangidae, Chanidae, Lutjanidae, and Serranidae. Results showed that Scombridae, Carangidae, and Chanidae accounted for approximately 67.7 percent of the total fish volume sold in the surveyed markets. These families play a significant role in supporting the fisheries economy and meeting consumer demand for seafood products.

The researchers also documented fish species with conservation concerns, including the endangered Humphead Wrasse (Cheilinus undulatus), the vulnerable Brown-Marbled Grouper (Epinephelus fuscoguttatus), and several near-threatened species. Their presence in public markets highlights the need for effective monitoring and documentation systems to support fisheries management, species conservation, and regulatory compliance. Accurate monitoring of fish species can assist authorities in identifying potential threats to marine biodiversity and implementing appropriate conservation measures.

In addition, the study found significant variations in fish prices among different habitat-associated groups. Reef-associated fishes were observed to have higher market values compared to other fish groups because of their commercial importance and consumer demand. The researchers concluded that information regarding fish species composition, conservation status, market prices, and supply sources provides valuable insights for fisheries management and decision-making.

These findings support the proposed FishLedger system by emphasizing the importance of collecting and managing fish-related information in a centralized platform. Through digital monitoring, supply source identification, automated reporting, and predictive analytics, the system can contribute to improved fish market management, enhanced transparency, and data-driven decision-making within the Lucena Fish Port Complex.

Fish Market Monitoring Systems in the Modern Day

Modern fish markets are gradually transitioning from traditional manual processes to digital monitoring systems. According to Cruz (2022), fish market monitoring systems are digital platforms designed to track fish transactions, inventory, and pricing, improving transparency and operational efficiency. In his study on “Digital Transformation in Coastal Market Systems,” it was revealed that manual recording methods in fish ports often lead to data inconsistencies, delays, and lack of transparency.

Similarly, Santos et al. (2023) found that the implementation of digital monitoring systems in fish ports increased operational efficiency by reducing reporting time and improving data accuracy. Their study highlighted that over seventy percent (70%) of fish market stakeholders preferred digital systems due to faster transaction recording and monitoring capabilities.

These findings support the goal of the present study, which aims to develop a centralized monitoring system for the Lucena Fish Port Complex. The system is expected to improve transaction identification, enhance transparency, and provide data access for stakeholders.

Progressive Web Applications in Information Systems

Progressive Web Applications (PWA) have emerged as a modern solution for delivering reliable, efficient, and accessible web-based systems. According to Sharma (2021), PWAs combine the functionality of both web and mobile applications, enabling users to access systems offline, receive updates, and experience improved performance across various devices. This hybrid capability makes PWAs highly suitable for environments where consistent internet connectivity may not always be available.

Garcia and Lee (2022), in their study “Adoption of Progressive Web Applications in Enterprise Systems,” revealed that PWAs significantly reduce development and maintenance costs while maintaining high performance and accessibility. Their findings showed that organizations adopting PWAs experienced improved user engagement, faster loading times, and enhanced system reliability. Additionally, PWAs eliminate the need for separate mobile applications, allowing businesses to streamline their digital infrastructure while still providing a mobile-friendly experience.

Furthermore, a report by Tech Insights (2023) indicated that more than sixty percent (60%) of businesses have shifted toward PWA-based systems due to their scalability, cross-platform compatibility, and ease of deployment. The report also emphasized that PWAs contribute to increased operational efficiency by simplifying updates and reducing dependency on platform-specific development.

These studies collectively justify the use of a Progressive Web Application in the FishLedger system, as it ensures accessibility, flexibility, and efficiency for fish vendors, administrators, and stakeholders. By leveraging PWA technology, the system can

function effectively even in areas with limited internet connectivity, while still providing a responsive and user-friendly experience.





Fish Supply Chain Identification and Traceability Systems

Traceability systems play a critical role in ensuring food safety, quality assurance, and transparency in the fisheries sector. According to Reyes (2024), fish supply chain identification systems enable stakeholders to monitor the movement of fish products from the point of catch to the final market destination, ensuring accountability and reducing the risk of fraud and mislabeling. These systems are essential in maintaining accurate records and promoting trust among consumers, suppliers, and regulatory agencies.

In a related study, Villanueva et al. (2022) developed a traceability system utilizing QR codes and mobile technology, allowing users to access detailed information 

about fish origin, handling processes, and distribution channels. The study revealed that such systems significantly improved consumer trust, strengthened regulatory compliance, and enhanced transparency within the supply chain. It also highlighted that digital traceability minimizes manual errors and simplifies data verification processes.

Additionally, a global review by Ahmed (2023) emphasized that traceability technologies, including Internet of Things (IoT) devices and digital databases, greatly enhance supply chain visibility and operational efficiency. The study pointed out that integrating these technologies allows data collection and monitoring, which supports better decision-making and more efficient resource management within the fisheries sector.

These findings support the inclusion of a supply source identification feature in the FishLedger system, which aims to provide accurate, reliable, and transparent

information about fish products within the Lucena Fish Port Complex. By integrating traceability functionalities, the system can improve accountability, strengthen stakeholder confidence, and ensure better management of fish supply chains.

Predictive analytics is widely used to forecast trends and improve decision-making across various industries. By analyzing past patterns and behaviors, organizations can make informed decisions, reduce uncertainties, and improve overall operational efficiency.

As a result, businesses are able to optimize inventory management, improve resource allocation, and respond more effectively to market fluctuations.

These studies collectively prove that predictive analytics can significantly enhance the FishLedger system by enabling accurate demand forecasting, improving supply planning, and supporting data-driven decision-making within the Lucena Fish Port Complex. Synthesis

The related literature and studies that were reviewed highlighted the growing importance of fish market monitoring systems, the effectiveness of progressive web applications (PWA), the role of supply chain traceability, the efficiency of automated reporting systems, and the increasing significance of predictive analytics in enhancing market operations and decision-making processes.

Fish market monitoring systems play a crucial role in improving transparency, efficiency, and accuracy in market operations by replacing traditional manual processes with digital solutions. These systems enable identification of transactions, pricing, and inventory, which are essential in maintaining reliable market data. However, challenges such as limited technological infrastructure, resistance to digital adoption among stakeholders, inconsistent data recording practices, and lack of centralized systems continue to hinder the full implementation of digital monitoring in fish ports. These issues are further compounded by operational inefficiencies, including delayed reporting, data redundancy, and difficulty in accessing historical records. To address these 

challenges, the integration of a centralized and user-friendly monitoring system is necessary to streamline operations and ensure data accuracy within fish market environments.

The reviewed literature also supports the use of Progressive Web Applications (PWA) as an effective platform for delivering accessible and scalable information systems. PWAs provide advantages such as offline functionality, cross-platform compatibility, and improved system performance, making them suitable for environments with unstable internet connectivity. Despite these benefits, some limitations remain, including low awareness among users, initial adaptation challenges, and the need for proper system design to ensure usability. Nevertheless, PWAs offer a practical solution for implementing digital systems in fish port operations, as they allow stakeholders to access information anytime and anywhere while maintaining system efficiency and reliability.

Supply chain identification and traceability systems were also identified as essential components in modern fish market operations. These systems enhance transparency by allowing stakeholders to track the origin and movement of fish products from source to market. They also improve accountability, food safety, and consumer trust. However, challenges such as fragmented data systems, lack of integration among stakeholders, and limited technological resources affect the effectiveness of traceability systems. Addressing these issues requires the development of an integrated platform that combines monitoring and identification functionalities to provide comprehensive and accurate supply chain information.

Predictive analytics was identified as a key driver in optimizing market strategies and improving decision-making. Studies have shown that predictive models using machine learning algorithms such as Decision Tree, Random Forest,  and Logistic Regression can effectively forecast demand, identify trends, and optimize resource allocation. These tools enable proactive planning and help organizations respond to potential challenges before they occur. This highlights the need for systems that incorporate predictive capabilities to maximize the value of collected data.

The foreign and local studies gathered by the researchers have been instrumental in building the foundation for the proposed study entitled “FishLedger: A Progressive Web Application Fish Market Monitoring System with Retailer Information, Supply Source Identification , Automated Reports, and Predictive Analytics for Lucena Fish Port Complex.” The proponents utilized the reviewed literature to conceptualize a solution that addresses the existing gaps in fish market operations, particularly the lack of integration among monitoring, identification , reporting, and analytics systems. These insights significantly influenced the design and development of a comprehensive platform that aims to improve efficiency, enhance transparency, and support data-driven decision-making within the Lucena Fish Port Complex.

Software Development Lifecycle



Figure 1. Agile Scrum Model for Fishledger

The study will utilize the Agile Scrum Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) in developing FishLedger. Agile Scrum is an iterative and incremental software development framework that emphasizes collaboration, adaptability, continuous improvement, and regular feedback from stakeholders throughout the development process.

The Agile Scrum process consists of the following phases:

Requirements. In this phase, the researchers will gather and identify the system requirements for FishLedger. Information will be collected through interviews, observations, and document analysis conducted at the Lucena Fish Port Complex. The gathered requirements will serve as the foundation for the features and functionalities of the proposed system, including retailer information management, fish arrival monitoring, supply source identification, automated report generation, dashboard visualization, and predictive analytics. The needs of the intended users will be analyzed to ensure that the system effectively addresses existing operational challenges.

Planning. In this phase, the researchers will plan the overall objectives, scope, and major functionalities of the system. Meetings and consultations with fish port administrators, personnel, retailers, and other stakeholders will be conducted to identify operational issues and determine the most appropriate solutions. The system architecture, database structure, user interface design, and sprint schedules will also be prepared during this phase.

Implementation. In this phase, the researchers will develop the FishLedger system based on the identified requirements and project plan. The selected features and modules will be coded, integrated, and developed incrementally throughout the sprint cycles. Core functionalities such as retailer registration, fish arrival monitoring, supply source identification, automated reporting, dashboard visualization, and predictive analytics will be implemented during this stage.

Test. In this phase, the researchers will conduct various testing procedures to evaluate the functionality, reliability, performance, and accuracy of the system. Unit testing, integration testing, and system testing will be performed to identify bugs, errors, or inconsistencies. Any issues discovered during testing will be corrected and improved before proceeding to deployment.

Weekly Scrum. In this phase, the researchers will conduct weekly Scrum meetings to monitor project progress, discuss completed tasks, identify challenges encountered during development, and plan upcoming activities. These meetings will ensure continuous communication among team members and maintain alignment with the project objectives.

Sprint Review. In this phase, the researchers will evaluate the completed functionalities developed during each sprint. The developed features will be demonstrated to stakeholders to gather feedback and determine whether the sprint goals have been achieved. Suggestions and recommendations obtained during this phase will be considered for future improvements and succeeding sprint cycles.

Sprint Retrospective. In this phase, the researchers will assess the overall performance of the completed sprint. The team will identify successful practices, challenges encountered, and areas requiring improvement. Lessons learned during the sprint will be documented and used to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of future development activities.

Deployment. Once the system has successfully passed the testing, review, and evaluation phases, it will be deployed for implementation at the Lucena Fish Port Complex. The developed system will be made available to authorized users, including fish port administrators, personnel, and stakeholders. Continuous enhancements, maintenance, and feature improvements may be implemented through future sprint iterations to ensure the system remains effective and responsive to user needs.

ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Evaluation

The developed FishLedger system will be evaluated using the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Model, an internationally recognized standard for assessing software quality. This model provides a comprehensive framework for measuring the quality and effectiveness of software systems based on specific quality characteristics. The evaluation will be conducted through a survey questionnaire administered to fish port administrators, personnel, and retailers who will use the system.

The evaluation will focus on the following quality characteristics:

1. Functional Suitability

Functional Suitability refers to the degree to which the system provides functions that meet the stated and implied needs of users when used under specified conditions.

Functional Completeness – The system provides all necessary functions required for fish market monitoring, retailer management, supply source identification, automated reporting, and predictive analytics.

Functional Correctness – The system produces accurate and correct results based on user inputs and stored data.

Functional Appropriateness – The functions of the system effectively support users in accomplishing their tasks efficiently.

2. Performance Efficiency

Performance Efficiency refers to the capability of the system to provide appropriate performance relative to the amount of resources used under stated conditions.

Time Behavior – The system responds quickly during data entry, retrieval, report generation, and dashboard loading.

Resource Utilization – The system efficiently uses available resources such as memory, processing power, and network connectivity.

Capacity – The system can handle multiple users and large volumes of fishmarket records without performance degradation.

 

3. Compatibility

Compatibility refers to the degree to which the system can operate effectively while sharing resources and exchanging information with other systems.

Co-existence – The system can operate alongside other software applications without conflicts.

Interoperability – The system can exchange and utilize information with external services and integrated technologies when necessary.

4. Usability

Usability refers to the degree to which the system can be used by specified users to achieve specified goals effectively, efficiently, and satisfactorily.

Appropriateness Recognizability – Users can easily recognize how the system can help them accomplish their tasks.

Learnability – The system is easy to learn, even for first-time users.

Operability – Users can easily navigate and control the system functions.

User Interface Aesthetics – The interface is visually appealing, organized, and understandable.

Accessibility – The system can be accessed and used across different devices and user environments.

5. Reliability

Reliability refers to the ability of the system to perform specified functions consistently under specified conditions for a specified period.

Maturity – The system performs consistently with minimal failures during operation.

Availability – The system remains accessible whenever needed by users.

Fault Tolerance – The system continues to function properly despite minor errors or unexpected conditions.

Recoverability – The system can restore data and resume operations after interruptions or failures.

6. Security

Security refers to the degree to which the system protects information and data so that users have appropriate access according to their authorization levels.

Confidentiality – Sensitive information is protected from unauthorized access.

Integrity – Data remains accurate, complete, and protected from unauthorized modification.

Accountability – User activities can be traced through system logs and records.

Authenticity – The system verifies the identity of users before granting access.
